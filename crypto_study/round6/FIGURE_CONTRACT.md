# Three-platform lifecycle figure and comparison tables

## Core conclusion

The registered small-bundle writer-lifecycle criterion fails in the Windows/i5 execution and passes in both Linux executions, while the full condition-level distributions show that Reset does not improve every larger-bundle condition.

## Evidence chain and composition

Use a quantitative grid with three platform rows (Win/i5, Linux/w5, Linux/Gold) and three representation columns (ordinary, compact, dictionary). Equal panels are required for direct comparison of the same conditions across the three observed environments. Every panel contains all 64 domain-specific conditions, for 576 points overall. No observations or conditions are selected or hidden.

Each point is the frozen cell median of 63 within-host paired Reset/fresh total-time ratios: three bundles by 21 technical repetitions. It is not a ratio of median times. Development and confirmation use distinct marker shapes and restrained blue/orange outlines. At each query count, fixed horizontal offsets expose the eight population, query-kind and locality conditions without implying a numeric horizontal measurement.

The shared vertical axis will be chosen only after reading the minimum and maximum of all 576 actual values. All nine panels must use the same limits and contain every point with visible margin. Lines mark parity at 1.00 and the development threshold at 0.90. The gate covers q = 1 and 8, requiring development ratios <= 0.90 and confirmation ratios < 1.00; q = 32 and 128 are descriptive.

A nine-row lifecycle table reports the eight-condition pass counts for each platform and representation at q = 1 and 8 in each domain. An eight-row range table reports the common worst-of-three complete-frame byte-ratio range and each host's range of cell medians of 63 within-host paired time ratios relative to ordinary transport. Endpoints may arise from different cells.
